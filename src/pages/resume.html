    Research

    • Analyzed DataUSA wage data for artists and related workers from 2014–2022; calculated women’s average earnings at 64–68% of men’s, showing the relative gap persisted despite rising wages for both groups.
    • Charted the wage gap directly as a ratio with its margin of error (±9–11 points per year), showing year-to-year changes fell within survey uncertainty.
    • Synthesized findings from NCES degree data, the Burns Halperin Report, and a PLOS ONE museum-collections study to trace women’s share from art degrees (61%) to museum exhibitions (15%), collections (13%), acquisitions (11%), and auction sales (3%).
    • Revised the original infographic after an accuracy audit: corrected a chart drawn out of proportion, replaced two unverifiable figures, and gave every statistic its year, population, and source. Code and data.
    • Completed before the 2026 FIFA World Cup, matching 2023–24 statistics from Europe’s five major leagues with historical World Cup squad records from 1930–2022, ending with the 2022 tournament; labels identify prior squad inclusion rather than predict 2026 selection.
    • Cleaned and matched player records by name, retaining 2,847 players and 30 numerical features; trained logistic regression and random forest classifiers with a stratified 80/20 training–test split.
    • Evaluated confusion matrices and recall alongside accuracy: approximately 82% accuracy fell below an always-not-selected baseline, while the models identified only 6 and 8 of 98 previously selected players in the test set.
    • Examined feature correlations and random-forest importance; documented class imbalance, name-matching limitations, and the timing mismatch between club statistics and historical squad labels. Notebook and report.
