Artist statement.

I have never been particularly comfortable with the familiar simply because it is familiar. Growing up in Bangladesh left me with a curiosity that rarely settles into one way of seeing. That restlessness still drives the work.

Painting is the center of my practice. I stay with subjects that reward close looking: horizons and changing light; architecture returned to over years; still lifes examined through observation, structure, and intuition; surfaces drawn from the natural world. These subjects interest me because they seem settled and ordinary, and therefore worth unsettling through sustained attention. My photographs extend that habit into daily life.

My training in mathematics shapes how I think about proportion, pattern, and spatial order. Art and mathematics are complementary perspectives. One clarifies and measures. The other stays with what resists quantification: ambiguity, atmosphere, and the imperfect mark.

The practice extends into ceramics, photography, print, and interactive work when a question needs another pace of looking. Across media, I am less interested in illustrating the world than in making a place where looking can keep going after recognition ends.
